Google Rolls Out AI Tools to Automate Marketing

Like Blog | Marketing

Google is pushing deeper into AI-powered advertising, and this time the changes are not subtle. Ahead of its annual Google Marketing Live event, the company has announced several new capabilities within AI Max its creative and targeting tool suite that automate everything from writing campaign briefs to selecting ad formats in real time. For marketers, the message is clear: the manual era of ad management is winding down.

What Is AI Max?

AI Max is Google's fastest-growing AI search product, according to Brendon Kraham, VP of Search and Commerce Global Ads Solutions. It sits within Google's broader advertising ecosystem as a tool focused specifically on search optimization helping advertisers reach the right people at the right moment with the right message, without requiring the same level of manual input that campaign management has historically demanded.

The new wave of features announced ahead of Google Marketing Live extends that vision significantly, touching creative writing, audience targeting, shopping ads, and vertical specific campaigns. The future, as Kraham put it bluntly, "is definitely more automated."

AI Brief: Writing Campaign Strategy for You

The headline feature is AI Brief a tool that lets advertisers write a plain-language brief describing their creative vision and targeting guardrails, which the AI then uses to guide the entire campaign. This is a direct departure from the keyword lists and manual settings that have defined Google Ads management for over two decades.

Advertisers can tell the AI to avoid mentioning pricing, prioritize specific search contexts like "healthy pantry staples," or use the Audience Guidelines feature to define who should be served customized ads. The tool launches in English for AI Max search campaigns first, with plans to roll it into Performance Max and other campaign types down the line. For smaller teams and solo marketers, this alone changes what is operationally possible.

Shopping Ads Get Smarter

Google is also bringing AI Max to shopping campaigns, pulling directly from advertisers' Merchant Center product feeds. Three features are rolling out here. Text customization rewrites ad copy on the fly based on the shopper's query and detected intent. Final URL expansion automatically routes users to whichever brand landing page is most relevant to what they searched for. And Optimal Format Selection picks the ad format that best fits the moment.

Together, these changes mean a single product feed can power a more responsive, personalized shopping ad experience without a human making decisions at each step. The system reads the shopper, matches the format, and serves the message all in milliseconds.

Vertical-Specific Features

Google is also shipping features tailored to specific industries. Regulated sectors like finance and healthcare get automatic text disclaimers that appear in ads even when Final URL expansion is active, ensuring compliance without manual overrides.

Travel advertisers get perhaps the most significant upgrade: unified search campaigns that bring together text ads, travel promotion ads, and booking links under a single AI Max umbrella with consolidated reporting. Kraham acknowledged that this area has historically been a pain point, describing the change as bringing together "what has been a disconnected set of tools." For travel brands juggling multiple campaign types, that consolidation alone is worth paying attention to.

What This Means for Marketers

The direction Google is moving in is not ambiguous. Search is becoming more conversational, more visual, and more personalized driven by AI agents and changing user behavior. Advertisers who rely on static keyword lists and manually written ad copy are going to find themselves increasingly outpaced by campaigns that adapt in real time.

That is both an opportunity and a displacement. The marketers who thrive in this environment will be the ones who understand how to brief AI systems well setting smart constraints, defining the right guardrails, and interpreting performance data to refine strategy. The tactical execution is being handed off. The strategic thinking is not. The job is not disappearing. It is changing shape faster than most job descriptions have caught up to.
