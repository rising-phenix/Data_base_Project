The Karachi Kid Who Built a $60 Billion AI Startup

Like Blog | Inspiration

Sualeh Asif grew up in Karachi solving math problems that most adults could not crack. By 26, he had co-founded Cursor one of the fastest-growing AI startups in the world, valued at nearly $10 billion, with a reported SpaceX acquisition deal on the table. His is not just a success story. It is a mirror held up to an entire generation of Pakistani talent wondering whether they have to leave to make it.

From Karachi Classrooms to MIT

Before Silicon Valley, there was a high school kid in Karachi who loved math more than almost anything else. Sualeh competed for Pakistan in the Asian Pacific Mathematical Olympiads in 2017 and 2018. He did not just participate he won praise and went on to represent Pakistan at the International Mathematical Olympiad. He also ran math camps, teaching younger students the same problem-solving frameworks that were quietly shaping his own mind.

That mind got him into MIT. He graduated in 2022 into an environment built entirely around bold ideas and the resources to chase them. The contrast with Pakistan's education system rigid, resource-starved, exam-focused could not have been sharper. MIT did not make Sualeh. But it gave him the runway he needed.

What Cursor Actually Is

In 2023, Sualeh co-founded Anysphere and took the lead at Cursor, an AI powered code editor built to make software development dramatically faster. Its standout feature, called Tab, does not just autocomplete code it predicts entire logical blocks, understanding context in a way earlier tools could not.

The product found its audience fast. Over a million developers now use Cursor, including engineers at Stripe, OpenAI, and some of the most demanding engineering teams in the world. It earns $100 million annually, raised $71 million in funding, and crossed unicorn status before most people had heard of it. Sualeh's personal net worth crossed $100 million before he turned 26.

The SpaceX Deal and What It Signals

Reports of a potential SpaceX acquisition have placed Cursor's valuation conversation at the $60 billion range numbers that belong in the same sentence as the most consequential tech companies of the decade. Whether or not that deal closes, the signal is clear. Cursor is not a promising startup anymore. It is infrastructure for how professional software gets built.

The speed of that trajectory from founding in 2023 to acquisition-level conversations in under two years is extraordinary by any standard. It is the kind of growth that rewrites what people believe is possible.

A Pakistani Win Built Outside Pakistan

Pakistanis celebrated Sualeh online with genuine pride. But the uncomfortable truth underneath the celebration is hard to ignore. His success did not come from Pakistan's system it came despite it. He left after high school. The funding, the network, the infrastructure, the mentorship none of it came from home. He had to cross an ocean to find the conditions his talent deserved.

Pakistan has over 17,000 startups and a young, tech hungry population of nearly 19 million middle and upper class consumers. But in 2024, startup funding fell 70 percent to just $22.5 million. Spotty electricity, weak internet, outdated curricula, and a thin investment ecosystem keep pushing its best people out. Sualeh's story is proof of what Pakistan produces. It is also a question Pakistan needs to answer.

What Comes Next

Cursor is still early. AI coding tools are in the middle of reshaping the entire software industry and Cursor sits at the center of that shift. The companies and developers who adopt it now are not just saving time they are restructuring how their engineering teams work from the ground up.

And for the next generation of kids in Karachi who are good at math, who love solving hard problems, who stay up late because a challenge will not let them sleep Sualeh Asif's name is now on the list of things that prove it is possible. The question is whether the next one has to leave to find out.
